Methodology

How we know what we know.

Eichler Market's data rests on three pillars: public records, MLS signals, and verified owner input. We tell you which is which.

01.Public tax records

Every home sale is a matter of public record. We compile county Assessor-Recorder transaction data — sale prices, sale dates, APNs, and building square footage — and match each record to its Eichler community. These are the numbers underneath every "avg $/sf" and "last sale" figure on the site.

We do not publish current owner names on community pages. Public records include them, but exposing them publicly is a privacy cost without a benefit to the buyer.

02.MLS signals & Eichler verification

Homes actively listed on the regional MLS appear with their list price and status. The "Eichler" designation itself comes from MLS listing tags on sold properties — when an agent lists a home and tags it as an Eichler, that signal is recorded permanently against the address. Our database of 1,768 homes across 54 communities is built from a decade of these verified sale records, cross-referenced with documented Eichler tract maps and city planning archives.

This means our coverage skews toward homes that have sold in the modern MLS era. Eichlers held by the same family for 40+ years may not yet appear — if yours is one, add it and we'll verify it against tract records.

03.Off-market intelligence

Unlisted homes — owners who'd sell at the right price, who've set a Make Me Move figure, or who've received an offer they're considering — exist in every community. We surface them two ways.

Owner-initiated: a homeowner signs up and names a price. That figure is visible to buyers who submit a formal offer; it is not public, not on Zillow, and never creates a price history.

Buyer-initiated: a buyer submits an offer on any home, listed or not. We route it to the owner through standard channels and track the response privately in the buyer's dashboard.

Why "flat 3%" works

The typical 5–6% sell-side commission on a $3M Eichler is $150–180K split across agents. That's a lot of friction on a transaction where the buyer pool is highly specific and often already watching the neighborhood. Our flat 3% covers the full transaction — offer management, escrow, closing, disclosures. Owners keep more; buyers know the number.
